I made this using [FL Studio!](/posts/switching-back-to-fl-studio)

Lately I've been listening to a lot of early [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem), especially tracks from *[The Marshall Mathers LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marshall_Mathers_LP)*, *[The Slim Shady LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Slim_Shady_LP)*, and [D12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D12) records like *[Purple Pills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Pills)*. The production from Eminem and [Jeff Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bass) really stands out. Those beats had this strange balance of being goofy, unsettling, and incredibly catchy.

I wanted to see if I could capture that feeling without recreating one of those records.

## Finding the Sound

Unlike some of my other tracks, this one wasn't built around a sample or a [bass line.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassline)

It started with the main [melody.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melody) As soon as I had that bouncing around in a loop, I knew it felt like something that could have lived somewhere in the early 2000s. Instead of trying to modernize it, I leaned into it.

## The Drums

The [drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_kit) in these old tracks are surprisingly simple when Eminem is [programming.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_(music))

Nothing feels overproduced but every hit lands exactly where it should. I added a little of my own flair with some triple [hi-hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi-hat) hits.

I spent more time moving drum hits around than adding new sounds. The groove is more important than complexity.

## Building the Track

The arrangement is a bit sparse to give the verses room.

Every few bars something changes, whether that's a percussion hit disappearing, a sound dropping out, or another layer quietly entering the mix. Those little changes keep the beat moving.

I also tried not to overcrowd it despite there being well over ten instruments on this.

One thing I admire about those early [Aftermath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_Entertainment) records is how much space they leave between sounds. It lets every instrument have its own personality.

## Looking Forward

I like beats that feel slightly off in a way that's fun. I think there's a lot more room to explore that direction, and it's probably closer to my own style than trying to chase whatever happens to be popular at the moment.
