## Two Treadmills, One Machine

It feels like the [rat race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_race) and [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media) are two separate problems. One is about [money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money) and [career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career), the other about [attention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention) and [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine).

---

## The Moving Goalpost

Here's how things work. Often, once you get a raise you feel like you've won. Like most people, your [lifestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifestyle) expands to swallow it, a phenomenon economists have a name for, [hedonic adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonic_treadmill). Even if the job title changes, the [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety) does not.

What nobody tells you at 22 is that the ladder doesn't have a top rung. It just has more rungs. I've met millionaires who were embarrassed in Florida because their yacht looked small compared to the billionaires who were parked there.

---

## The Feed That Never Ends

Social media runs an identical [con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_trick). [The infinite scroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_scroll) was not designed by accident. The [algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithm) behind it is tuned by people who understand [variable ratio reinforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement#Schedules_of_reinforcement) better than many professional [psychologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology). This is the same mechanism that makes [slot machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slot_machine) so hard to walk away from.

It is important to understand that the [reward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reward_system) isn't the content itself, but the anticipation of what comes next.[^1]

There is no "you've reached the end of the internet" screen. If there are eyes on a feed, there are eyes to advertise to.

---

## Why & What We Don't Notice

Both systems survive by keeping the finish line just far enough away. 

If the ladder or the feed ever felt truly [pointless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism), people would step off.

I think this is why burnout from a [career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career) and burnout from your [phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) feel similar. They both leave you exhausted from chasing *something*.

---

## Stepping Off Instead Of Speeding Up

The move is to decide, in advance, what *enough* looks like for you before a machine or company defines it for you. 

Think of a [job title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_title) you wouldn't trade your evenings for. Create healthy social circles and hobbies that are more interesting to engage with than social media.

---

## Conclusion

Decide what enough looks like before something else decides it for you.

[^1]: This idea draws on the work of neuroscientist [Wolfram Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Schultz), whose research showed that dopamine neurons respond to differences between expected and actual rewards. This is a phenomenon known as **reward prediction error**.
