I made this using [FL Studio!](/posts/switching-back-to-fl-studio)

After finishing [*Villains*](/posts/making-villains), I wanted to go in the opposite direction. Instead of making something weird and bouncy, I wanted something that felt reflective.

I've always loved the atmosphere on old boom bap records. Producers like [J Dilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Dilla), [DJ Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Premier), and [Pete Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Rock) had a way of making simple loops feel alive. I wasn't trying to copy any one producer, but I definitely had that sound in the back of my mind.

## The Drums

I took a bit of time doing the hi-hats on this so there is a solid swing to it.

Rather than making the drums loud or aggressive, I wanted them to sit inside the sample and keep everything moving. I spent a lot of time nudging individual hits around until the groove felt natural and not too repetitive.

## Building the Track

I tried to leave as much space as possible.

It's always tempting to keep adding instruments because modern software gives you unlimited options. Instead, I added different chops, small percussion changes, and little moments where instruments quietly enter or disappear. These details keep the beat moving without drawing too much attention.

I wanted the whole thing to feel like a soundtrack to a quiet drive through streets that are usually busy during the day but completely empty at night.

## Looking Forward

I'm finding that I enjoy making boom bap much more than chasing whatever sound happens to be popular. There's something satisfying about building a beat that rewards you for listening closely.
